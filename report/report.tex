\begin{abstract}
  We predict coronary heart disease (MICHD) from 321 answers of the BRFSS
  2015 health survey. The task is imbalanced (8.8\% positives) and the data
  have structured missing values and codebook special codes. We clean the
  data column by column, compare the linear methods of the course with a
  gradient boosted tree model (GBM) and a small neural network (MLP), and
  average the last two. The ensemble reaches a cross-validated F1 of 0.437,
  0.434 on a held-out set and 0.448 on AIcrowd. Seven survey questions asked
  after a cardiac event raise F1 by about 0.01. We exclude them from the
  official model, because they are not available when screening
  undiagnosed people.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System (BRFSS) is a yearly US
telephone survey on health and behaviour \cite{brfss2015}. The label
\texttt{\_MICHD} states whether a respondent reported coronary heart disease
or a myocardial infarction. The data have 328,135 training rows, 109,379
test rows and 321 features. Only 8.83\% of the labels are positive.
Predicting $-1$ for everyone gives 91.2\% accuracy but F1 $=0$, so we
optimize the F1 of the positive class. Only NumPy is allowed. Our
contributions are (i) codebook-driven cleaning, (ii) a NumPy GBM and MLP
ensemble, and (iii) an analysis of leaky survey questions.

\section{Data and preprocessing}
\label{sec:data}
\textbf{Special codes.} The 2015 codebook \cite{brfss2015codebook} defines
special codes per column. Codes 7/9, 77/99 and 777/999 (``don't know'',
``refused'') become missing; 88 and 888 (``none'') become 0. The rule is set
per column, because 7 or 99 are valid values in some columns. Weight and
height are converted to kg and cm, and frequency codes to one unit.

\textbf{Missing values.} 147 of the 321 columns are more than 50\% missing.
Most gaps follow the survey skip logic and are not random. Eleven
skip-logic fills set the implied answer, e.g.\ ``takes blood pressure
medication'' $=$ no when the person has no high blood pressure. Missingness
is informative: the positive rate is 3.8\% when \texttt{BPMEDS} is missing
and 16.3\% when it is observed. We therefore add missing-value indicators
and one ``don't know'' flag per question.

\textbf{Column selection.} We drop survey administration fields, columns
more than 95\% empty and redundant recodes. The cleaned matrix keeps 75 raw
and 2 derived features plus 61 ``don't know'' flags (138 columns).

\textbf{Fitted pipeline.} Median or mode imputation, one-hot encoding of
nominal columns (rare levels pooled), $\log(1+x)$ of counts and
standardization give about 140 columns. All statistics are fitted on the
training folds only.

\section{Models and validation}
\label{sec:models}
\textbf{Validation protocol.} We hold out a stratified 20\% of the training
rows (65,627 rows, seed 2026). All choices use 5-fold stratified
cross-validation (CV) on the remaining 262,508 development rows.
Preprocessing, the state encoding, the models and the decision rule are
fitted inside each fold. A change is accepted only if mean F1 rises by at
least 0.001 and it wins on at least 4 of 5 folds. No choice uses AIcrowd or
the test set.

\textbf{Linear baselines.} We train the six methods of
\texttt{implementations.py} on the fitted pipeline with 10 pairwise
interactions. Labels are $\pm1$ for squared loss and $\{0,1\}$ for the
logistic loss
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}(w)=\tfrac{1}{N}\textstyle\sum_{n}
  \left[\log(1+e^{x_n^\top w})-y_n x_n^\top w\right]+\lambda\|w\|^2 .
\end{equation}
With the default threshold the logistic model has F1 0.22. Every linear
model needs a tuned decision rule.

\textbf{Gradient boosted trees.} Our NumPy GBM minimizes the logistic loss
with second-order (Newton) leaf values $-G/(H+\lambda)$. Features are binned
into 64 histogram bins. Missing values form their own bin, and each split
learns where to send them. Trees have depth 5, learning rate 0.05, row and
column subsampling 0.7 and 0.8, at least 200 rows per leaf and $\lambda=5$.
Early stopping uses an inner 20\% split. The state of residence is replaced
by its smoothed prevalence, fitted on the training folds (+0.0026 F1, 4/5
folds).

\textbf{Neural network.} The MLP has one hidden layer of 64 ReLU units. It is
trained with Adam (learning rate $10^{-3}$, batch 512), weight decay
$10^{-3}$ and early stopping. We average the logits of 10 seeds.

\textbf{Ensemble and decision rule.} The final score is
$0.5\,s_{\text{GBM}}+0.5\,s_{\text{MLP}}$ (logits). We predict $+1$ for the
14\% of rows with the highest score. The share is more robust than a fixed
threshold when the score scale changes after retraining.
Fig.~\ref{fig:share} shows that F1 is flat between 12\% and 16\%.

\section{Results}
Table~\ref{tab:results} compares all models on the same folds. Squared-loss
methods reach 0.422. The logistic loss adds 0.008. Both non-linear models
beat the linear baselines, and their average is best. The out-of-fold
scores of the GBM and the MLP are correlated at 0.976, which limits the gain
of the ensemble.

On the held-out rows the final model has precision 0.354 and recall 0.561.
On AIcrowd it scores F1 0.448 and accuracy 87.8\%. The gap with CV is about
two bootstrap standard deviations of the public F1 (sd 0.005).

\textbf{Rejected alternatives.} None of the following passed the acceptance
rule: class weights ($-0.003$) and focal loss ($-0.021$); PCA (0.403--0.413
with logistic regression); feature selection; extra composite features
($-0.0007$ to $-0.0009$); crossed target encodings ($-0.0010$ to $-0.0013$);
refitting the GBM on 100\% of the rows ($-0.0010$); deeper trees and
networks; Naive Bayes ($-0.016$), random forest and $k$-NN members; stacking;
and per-group shares by sex or age ($-0.001$). Gains below 0.002 were within
seed noise.

\textbf{Important features.} The largest drops in AUC when a feature is
permuted come from age (0.035), general health (0.021), sex (0.018), high
cholesterol (0.009) and prior stroke (0.008). Simple rules agree: a prior
stroke has a lift of 4.24, and five or more chronic conditions a lift of 3.72.

\section{Leaky questions}
Seven questions are asked after a cardiac event or concern its treatment:
cardiac and stroke rehabilitation (\texttt{HAREHAB1}, \texttt{STREHAB1}),
and aspirin use and its reasons (\texttt{CVDASPRN}, \texttt{RDUCHART},
\texttt{RDUCSTRK}, \texttt{RLIVPAIN}, \texttt{ASPUNSAF}). Their strength
differs. All 615 development respondents who answered \texttt{HAREHAB1} are
positive (158 of 158 in the held-out set). \texttt{STREHAB1} has a positive
rate of 36\%, the other five 5--21\%. Such features are a form of target
leakage \cite{kaufman2012leakage}.

Table~\ref{tab:leak} shows that adding them improves every model by 0.009
to 0.012, on 5 of 5 folds. \texttt{HAREHAB1} alone gives +0.008, and the
aspirin items +0.004 more. The leaky ensemble scores 0.457 on AIcrowd.

The gain is real for this benchmark, but it depends on the context of use.
A screening model is meant for people without a diagnosis. These people
have never been offered cardiac rehabilitation, so the answer that drives
the gain does not exist for them. We therefore keep the clean model as our
official submission and report the leaky variant separately
(\texttt{run.py --with\_leakage}).

\section{Discussion}
All model families reach a similar F1 of 0.42--0.44, which suggests a
ceiling for self-reported survey data. Most errors are ``silent'' cases. Of
the false negatives, 56\% are women, 40\% have no high blood pressure, 36\%
report excellent or very good health and 96\% report no stroke. These
respondents give the survey little signal. Better recall would need
clinical measures such as blood pressure, lipids or an ECG. The public
leaderboard is also noisy: a 0.5-point change in prevalence moves F1 by
about 0.01.

\section{Summary}
A NumPy ensemble of gradient boosted trees and a neural network, with
careful codebook-based cleaning, reaches F1 0.434 on held-out data and 0.448
on AIcrowd. Questions asked after a cardiac event add about 0.01 F1. We
exclude them, because they would not exist when screening undiagnosed
people.
