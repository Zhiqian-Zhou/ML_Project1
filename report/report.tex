\title{Predicting Coronary Heart Disease from the BRFSS 2015 Survey\\
  with Gradient Boosting and Neural Networks in Pure NumPy}

\author{

  \textit{School of Computer and Communication Sciences, EPFL, Switzerland}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
  We predict coronary heart disease (\texttt{\_MICHD}) from 321 BRFSS 2015 survey
  features ($N=328,135$, 8.83\% positive). Naive negative classification yields
  91.17\% accuracy but trivial $F_1=0$, and uncalibrated linear models exhibit severe
  false-negative bias ($F_1 \le 0.23$). Operating threshold calibration provides an
  immediate +85\% relative leap ($F_1 \to 0.428$). We develop a pure-NumPy ensemble
  combining Histogram Gradient Boosted Trees (GBM) with second-order leaf updates
  and an Adam-trained Multi-Seed Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP). The clean ensemble
  achieves 5-fold CV $F_1$ of 0.437, 0.434 on holdout, and 0.448 on AIcrowd (87.8\%
  accuracy) with zero target leakage. Seven post-cardiac event questions inflate test
  $F_1$ to 0.457 (88.6\% accuracy), which we isolate to preserve clinical screening
  integrity while documenting benchmark leakage.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System (BRFSS) is a nationwide US
health survey \cite{brfss2015}. Target \texttt{\_MICHD} ($\pm 1$) records
reported coronary heart disease or myocardial infarction (328,135 training
rows, 109,379 test rows, 321 features). Only 8.83\% of labels are positive
($\sim 10:1$ skew). Under this imbalance, predicting $-1$ for all rows achieves
91.17\% accuracy yet yields $F_1 = 0$ \cite{vanrijsbergen1979}; positive-class
$F_1$ thus guides model selection and threshold calibration \cite{fawcett2006roc}.

Restricted to pure standard library and NumPy (no scikit-learn or PyTorch), our
contributions are: (i) codebook-driven cleaning and skip-logic imputation;
(ii) benchmarking six canonical linear methods \cite{cs433} and uncovering an
+85\% gain from threshold calibration; (iii) pure-NumPy Histogram GBMs
\cite{friedman2001greedy,chen2016xgboost} and multi-seed MLPs \cite{kingma2015adam};
and (iv) empirical dissection of target leakage \cite{kaufman2012leakage}.

\section{Data and Preprocessing}
\label{sec:data}
\textbf{Special sentinels.} The CDC codebook \cite{brfss2015codebook} specifies
sentinels: 7/9, 77/99, 777/999 (``don't know / refused'') map to NaN; 88 and 888
(``none / zero days / no alcohol'') map to 0. Weight and height are converted to
kg and cm, and frequency responses mapped to uniform units.

\textbf{Informative missingness \& skip logic.} 147 of 321 columns contain $>50\%$
missing values, following structured survey logic rather than MCAR. Eleven domain
skip-logic rules fill implied values (e.g., blood pressure medication \texttt{BPMEDS}
filled with 0 for non-hypertensive subjects, where prevalence is 3.8\% vs.\ 16.3\%
when observed). We append missingness masks and ``don't know'' indicator flags.

\textbf{Column selection \& pipeline.} Pruning administrative columns and features
$>95\%$ empty leaves 75 raw features and 61 indicator flags. The pipeline applies
median/mode imputation, one-hot encoding of common levels, $\log(1+x)$ of counts,
clinical interactions (e.g., Blood Pressure $\times$ Cholesterol), and standard
scaling. All statistics are fitted strictly out-of-fold.

\section{Models and Validation Protocol}
\label{sec:models}
\textbf{Validation protocol.} We hold out a stratified 20\% split (65,627 rows,
seed 2026). All modeling decisions and hyperparameter searches use 5-fold
stratified cross-validation (CV) on the remaining 262,508 development rows.
Preprocessing, target encodings, and decision thresholds are fitted inside each
fold. A modification is accepted only if mean CV $F_1$ rises by $\ge 0.001$ and
wins on $\ge 4/5$ folds. No selection uses the test set or AIcrowd.

\textbf{Linear baselines \& calibration.} We train the six required methods
from \cite{cs433} on standardized features with 10 clinical interactions. Under
regularized logistic loss $\mathcal{L}(w)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^N
\left[\log(1+e^{x_n^\top w})-y_n x_n^\top w\right]+\lambda\|w\|_2^2$,
the default cutoff ($\hat{y} \ge 0.5$) yields $F_1 = 0.2311$ due to extreme
false-negative bias under the 8.83\% prior. Calibrating the decision cutoff
$\tau^* \approx 0.218$ yields an immediate +85\% relative surge to
$F_1 = 0.428-0.430$ \cite{fawcett2006roc}.

\textbf{Histogram GBM.} Our pure-NumPy tree booster minimizes logistic loss
with second-order Newton leaf updates $\Delta w = -G/(H+\lambda)$
\cite{chen2016xgboost}. Features are binned into 64 histogram bins. Missing
values form a dedicated bin, with each split learning the optimal descent
direction. Hyperparameters: depth 5, learning rate 0.05, row/column subsampling
0.7/0.8, min 200 leaf samples, and $\lambda=5.0$. Early stopping evaluates an
inner 20\% split. State of residence (\texttt{\_STATE}) is encoded via empirical
Bayes smoothed target prevalence (+0.0026 $F_1$, 4/5 folds). Bagged over 3 seeds.

\textbf{Neural network.} The MLP features a 64-unit ReLU hidden layer, trained
with Adam \cite{kingma2015adam} (learning rate $10^{-3}$, batch size 512, weight
decay $10^{-3}$) and inner early stopping. Output logits are averaged across 10
distinct random seeds to suppress stochastic initialization variance.

\textbf{Ensemble \& decision rule.} The ensemble blends logits:
$s = 0.5\,s_{\text{GBM}} + 0.5\,s_{\text{MLP}}$. We assign $+1$ to the top
13.6\%--14.0\% highest scores. Percentile thresholding is more invariant to
post-retraining score scale drift than a static cutoff. Fig.~\ref{fig:share}
illustrates that $F_1$ forms a stable plateau between 12\% and 16\%.

On held-out rows, the clean ensemble achieves precision 0.354, recall 0.561,
and $F_1 = 0.4340$. On AIcrowd, it scores $F_1 = 0.448$ and 87.8\% accuracy.
The gap with CV is within two bootstrap standard deviations ($\sigma \approx 0.005$).

\textbf{Rejected alternatives \& coverage analysis.} The following failed our
acceptance rule: class weighting ($-0.003$), focal loss ($-0.021$), PCA
($0.403$--$0.413$), feature selection, crossed target encodings, full-row GBM
refitting ($-0.0010$), seed-diversified splits (\texttt{split\_per\_seed}, paired
bootstrap intervals crossed zero), stacking, Naive Bayes ($-0.016$), and group
thresholds ($-0.001$).

\textbf{Feature importance.} Permutation AUC drops identify primary predictors:
age (0.035), general health (0.021), sex (0.018), high cholesterol (0.009),
and prior stroke (0.008, empirical odds lift 4.24).

\section{Target Leakage Analysis}
Seven questions relate strictly to post-cardiac events or subsequent therapy:
cardiac and stroke rehabilitation (\texttt{HAREHAB1}, \texttt{STREHAB1}), and
prescribed aspirin regimen (\texttt{CVDASPRN}, \texttt{RDUCHART}, \texttt{RDUCSTRK},
\texttt{RLIVPAIN}, \texttt{ASPUNSAF}). Their predictive power represents target
leakage \cite{kaufman2012leakage}: 100\% of respondents who answered
\texttt{HAREHAB1} (773/773 in training, 158/158 in held-out) test positive.
\texttt{STREHAB1} has a 36\% positive rate, and the aspirin indicators 5\%--21\%.

Table~\ref{tab:leak} demonstrates that including these questions lifts every
model by 0.009 to 0.012 $F_1$ across all 5 folds. The leaky ensemble attains
0.457 on AIcrowd (88.6\% accuracy).

However, screening models target undiagnosed individuals who cannot have
received cardiac rehabilitation; hence, these answers do not exist at inference
time. We therefore designate the zero-leakage model as our official submission
(\texttt{run.py}) and report the leaky variant separately
(\texttt{run.py --with\_leakage}) to transparently quantify benchmark leakage.

\section{Discussion}
All architectures plateau at $F_1 \approx 0.42$--$0.44$, highlighting the ceiling of
self-reported survey data. False negatives predominantly stem from asymptomatic
presentations: 56\% are female, 40\% non-hypertensive, 36\% report good/excellent
health, and 96\% have no stroke history. Detecting such silent cases requires
clinical biomarkers (troponin, ECG, lipid panels) unavailable in phone surveys.

\section{Summary}
Our pure-NumPy ensemble of Histogram Gradient Boosted Trees and an Adam-trained MLP,
backed by codebook cleaning and threshold calibration, reaches $F_1 = 0.434$ on holdout
and $0.448$ on AIcrowd (87.8\% accuracy). Post-cardiac event questions inflate $F_1$
to $0.457$ (88.6\% accuracy), which we isolate to preserve screening validity.
