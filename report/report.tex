\begin{abstract}
Predicting coronary heart disease (\texttt{\_MICHD}) from 321 BRFSS survey features is challenging due to extreme class imbalance (8.8\% positive) and non-random missingness. We develop an end-to-end classification pipeline strictly from scratch in pure NumPy. We resolve survey skip patterns via domain-guided imputation, benchmark the six course linear models against custom histogram-based gradient boosted trees (GBM) and neural networks (MLP), and optimize decision thresholds. Our final GBM-MLP ensemble achieves an F1 score of 0.447 (87.7\% accuracy) on AIcrowd, substantially outperforming linear baselines. Crucially, we uncover severe target leakage in seven post-event survey questions: while exploiting them inflates test F1 to 0.457 (88.3\% accuracy), we strictly exclude them from our primary model to ensure clinically sound pre-event screening.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Coronary heart disease is a leading global cause of death, motivating low-cost screening tools. The Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System (BRFSS) is an annual telephone survey assessing health conditions across the United States \cite{brfss2015}. The binary target \texttt{\_MICHD} indicates whether a respondent has experienced coronary heart disease or a heart attack. The dataset contains 328,135 training rows, 109,379 test rows, and 321 features. Predicting this outcome poses key challenges: extreme class imbalance (8.83\% positive), non-standard encodings (refusal and frequency codes), and widespread missingness. A majority-class baseline yields 91.2\% accuracy but zero F1; hence, all models are evaluated on positive-class F1 \cite{vanrijsbergen1979}. Constrained to pure NumPy implementations, our main contributions are: (i) codebook-informed preprocessing that resolves survey skip logic and missingness signals, (ii) a systematic benchmark of linear models, histogram-based gradient boosted trees (GBM), and neural networks (MLP) with calibrated decision thresholding, and (iii) the clinical discovery and audit of target leakage in post-event survey questions \cite{kaufman2012leakage}.

\section{Data and preprocessing}
\label{sec:data}
\textbf{Value and unit cleaning.} Following the CDC codebook \cite{brfss2015codebook}, sentinel value 88 (``none'') is mapped to 0, while 7 and 9 (``don't know''/``refused'') are treated as missing. Imperial and metric measurements (e.g., height, weight) are standardized to centimeters and kilograms.

\textbf{Skip patterns and non-response.} Over 45\% of questions are $>50\%$ missing due to survey skip patterns rather than random omissions. For example, blood pressure medication (\texttt{BPMEDS}) is asked only of hypertensive respondents (16.3\% prevalence vs.\ 3.8\% when skipped). We apply eleven domain rules to infer implied negatives (e.g., no hypertension implies no medication). Because respondents answering ``don't know'' exhibit higher disease rates, we extract 61 binary uncertainty indicators as predictive risk features.

\textbf{Feature selection and pipeline.} We remove 6 constant columns, administrative IDs, columns $>95\%$ missing, and 44 collinear pairs ($|r|>0.9$), retaining 75 raw features, 2 engineered metrics, and 61 uncertainty masks (138 total). Heavily skewed continuous variables are compressed via $\log(1+x)$. We then perform median/mode imputation, standardization, one-hot encoding, and add 10 clinical interaction terms. To prevent data leakage, all preprocessing statistics are estimated strictly inside training folds.

\section{Models and validation}
\label{sec:models}
\textbf{Validation protocol.} A stratified 20\% split (65,627 rows) is reserved as an independent held-out set. All model selection and hyperparameter tuning use 5-fold stratified cross-validation (CV) on the remaining 262,508 development rows.

\textbf{Linear baselines.} We evaluate the six course baseline methods in \texttt{implementations.py} \cite{cs433} on standardized features, using targets in $\pm1$ (mean squared error) and $\{0,1\}$ (logistic loss). Calibrated decision thresholds are essential under severe class imbalance.

\textbf{Gradient boosted trees.} Decision trees naturally capture non-linear interactions without requiring monotonic scaling. We implement a histogram-based gradient boosted tree (GBM) \cite{friedman2001greedy} in NumPy, minimizing logistic loss with L2-regularized Newton leaf steps ($\lambda=5$) \cite{chen2016xgboost}. Features are partitioned into 64 quantile bins with dedicated missing bins. The model uses depth-5 trees, learning rate 0.05, subsampling ratios of 0.7 (rows) and 0.8 (columns), target-encoded state codes \cite{miccibarreca2001}, and early stopping.

\textbf{Neural network.} An MLP with 64 ReLU hidden units is implemented in NumPy to provide smooth continuous decision boundaries. Trained with Adam \cite{kingma2015adam} (learning rate $10^{-3}$, batch size 512, weight decay $10^{-3}$), logits are averaged across 10 random seeds to eliminate initialization variance.

\textbf{Ensemble and decision rule.} We blend the tree ensemble and neural network by averaging raw output scores ($0.5\,s_{\text{GBM}}+0.5\,s_{\text{MLP}}$). Rather than a fixed 0.5 probability cutoff, we classify the top 14\% highest-risk individuals as positive. This percentile rule is invariant to calibration drift across folds; as shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:share}, F1 remains stable across positive rates of 12--16\%.

\textbf{Impact of decision thresholding.} On raw logistic predictions, the default 0.5 cutoff yields an F1 of only 0.2302. Selecting the top 14\% highest-risk individuals elevates F1 to 0.4302 (+0.200), marking the single largest performance jump in the pipeline.

\textbf{Impact of data preprocessing.} Sentinel correction (88 to 0, 7/9 to missing), skip-logic reconstruction, and log-scaling raise tuned logistic CV F1 from 0.4277 to 0.4311, providing a solid baseline for modeling.

\textbf{Model comparison and ensembling.} Table~\ref{tab:results} summarizes the benchmark. Least squares, ridge, and batch GD yield identical solutions ($0.4216 \pm 0.0034$), whereas SGD lags at 0.3853 due to stochastic noise under class skew. Logistic regression achieves 0.4300. Custom NumPy models surpass the linear ceiling: histogram GBM reaches CV F1 0.4354, and the 10-seed MLP reaches 0.4334. Their 50/50 blend achieves the best performance (CV F1 0.4368, held-out F1 0.4340), scoring 0.447 with 87.7\% accuracy on AIcrowd.

\textbf{Feature importance.} Test AUC permutation drops \cite{fawcett2006roc} identify the top risk factors: age (0.035 drop), general health (0.021), sex (0.018), high cholesterol (0.009), and prior stroke (0.008). Prior stroke confers a 4.24-fold risk lift, and $\ge 5$ chronic conditions carry a 3.72-fold lift.

\section{Target leakage analysis}
\label{sec:leakage}
Seven survey questions reference post-event care: cardiac and stroke rehabilitation (\texttt{HAREHAB1}, \texttt{STREHAB1}), daily aspirin use, and rationales (\texttt{CVDASPRN}, \texttt{RDUCHART}, \texttt{RDUCSTRK}, \texttt{RLIVPAIN}, \texttt{ASPUNSAF}). Notably, all 615 development respondents reporting cardiac rehabilitation had positive diagnoses (and 158/158 on held-out data), representing severe target leakage \cite{kaufman2012leakage}.

Including these features boosts F1 by $\approx 0.010$ across models (Table~\ref{tab:leak}), elevating AIcrowd test F1 to 0.457 (88.3\% accuracy). However, this reflects reverse causality: undiagnosed individuals are never prescribed cardiac rehabilitation. We exclude these leaky questions from our official model to preserve screening validity, while documenting the leaky variant as a benchmark (\texttt{python run.py --with\_leakage}).

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
All clean models plateau in the 0.42--0.44 F1 range, reflecting an information ceiling in self-reported survey data. Error analysis shows that false negatives are predominantly ``silent'' cases: 56\% female, 40\% without diagnosed hypertension, 36\% reporting excellent/good health, and 96\% without stroke history. Without classical risk factors, these cases offer negligible signal. Surpassing this ceiling requires clinical measurements (e.g., lipid panels, ECG). Moreover, benchmark scores show sample variance: a 0.5\% change in positive test prevalence shifts F1 by $\approx 0.01$, indicating that leaderboard differences partly reflect sampling noise.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
Built from scratch in pure NumPy, our ensemble combining codebook-guided preprocessing, histogram GBMs, and an MLP achieves 0.434 held-out F1 and 0.447 on AIcrowd (87.7\% accuracy). We identified severe reverse-causality leakage in seven post-event questions that inflates F1 to 0.457 (88.3\% accuracy), and strictly excluded them to deliver a clinically valid pre-event screening system.
